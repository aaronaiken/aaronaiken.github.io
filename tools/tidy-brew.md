<section class="tb-sec" id="what">
  <div class="tb-wrap">
    <p class="tb-eyebrow">What it does</p>
    <h2 class="tb-h2">One job: don't let the brew be forgotten.</h2>
    <ul class="tb-does">
      <li><b>Long steeps get watched.</b> Cold brew is a 12–24 hour timer that refuses to be forgotten — a lock-screen Live Activity, a heads-up an hour out, and a done alert that repeats every ten minutes until you say you pulled it.</li>
      <li><b>Every method, its own shape.</b> French press, AeroPress and tea are plain countdowns; pour over is a guided, pour-by-pour timer. Two can run at once — overnight cold brew while this morning's press steeps.</li>
      <li><b>It learns your cup.</b> Rate each brew weak, just right, or bitter. After a few, it notices which steep length you actually like and offers to make that your default.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="tb-sec">
  <div class="tb-wrap">
    <p class="tb-eyebrow">Phone &amp; wrist</p>
    <h2 class="tb-h2">Standalone on the watch, kept on the phone.</h2>
    <p class="tb-serif">The Apple Watch app runs short brews end to end on the wrist — start, a time-sensitive done alert, confirm, logged — with the phone off or absent. Cold brew stays on the phone, where a half-day timer belongs.</p>
    <p class="tb-note">Finished brews sync phone ↔ watch over your own private iCloud — never a running timer, only the ones you've pulled. The phone keeps the whole journal; the watch keeps a couple of months. No account, no analytics, and nowhere else for any of it to go.</p>
  </div>
</section>
