<section class="eb-sec">
  <div class="eb-wrap">
    <p class="eb-eyebrow">The whole idea</p>
    <p class="eb-quote">A mirror, not a <em>coach.</em></p>
    <p class="eb-body">Most health apps grade you. Ebb Life just reflects. You rate four parts of your day, it reads sleep and movement and heart rate from Apple Health, and it holds the two up side by side. <b>No streaks, no goals, nothing to break.</b> A day with three bourbons and four hours of sleep is data, not a failure — and a bad week is never something the app makes you feel.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="eb-sec" style="padding-top:0;">
  <div class="eb-wrap">
    <p class="eb-eyebrow">The four</p>
    <h2 class="eb-h2">Physical, mental, sexual, relational.</h2>
    <p class="eb-body">Four scores, every night. The last two are first-class here, not an afterthought — because they travel with sleep, drink, stress and movement as much as anything, and almost nothing measures them. Each domain has its own colour at the same weight as the others, so a low score looks exactly like a high one. Only the length changes.</p>
    <div class="eb-doms">
      <div class="eb-domcard"><div class="bar" style="background:var(--phys)"></div><h4>Physical</h4><p>Energy, vitality, soreness.</p></div>

<section class="eb-sec">
  <div class="eb-wrap">
    <p class="eb-eyebrow">How it works</p>
    <h2 class="eb-h2">You tap four things. It does the rest.</h2>
    <div class="eb-how">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">Each night</span>
        <h3>Score the four.</h3>
        <p>Four 1–10 sliders, in your own words — you write what each number means during setup, so a 7 means the same thing every night.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <span class="tag">On its own</span>
        <h3>Reads Apple Health.</h3>
        <p>Sleep, steps, workouts, resting heart rate, HRV, weight — read only. Drinks you log write back to Health (water, caffeine, alcohol) so you don't need a second app. Food is a few taps.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <span class="tag">In about three weeks</span>
        <h3>Shows what travels together.</h3>
        <p>Once there's enough to be sure, it starts naming the patterns — the honest ones, with the numbers behind them.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<hr class="eb-rule">

<section class="eb-sec">
  <div class="eb-wrap">
    <p class="eb-eyebrow">The payoff</p>
    <h2 class="eb-h2">Patterns, not verdicts.</h2>
    <p class="eb-body">After a few weeks Ebb Life starts telling you which of your own inputs move which scores — in plain language, ranked by how sure it is, with the working shown. It only speaks when the maths clears a conservative bar, it says <b>"not enough for that yet"</b> rather than inventing something, and it never claims cause. The cost of a wrong pattern is trust.</p>
    <div class="eb-insight">
      <p>Alcohol the night before is your strongest negative signal — across all four domains.</p>

<section class="eb-sec">
  <div class="eb-wrap">
    <p class="eb-eyebrow">Drinks</p>
    <h2 class="eb-h2">A drink tracker that writes to Health.</h2>
    <p class="eb-body">Build a shelf of what you actually drink — your coffee, your pour, your usual glass of water — each at its real size. Logging is one tap, or a long-press to change the size or count; nothing is ever typed. Alcohol shows the standard-drink maths (ml × ABV × 0.789 ⁄ 14), caffeine scales with the cup, and water reads against your own typical day, never a target. <b>The drinks you log write straight to Apple Health</b> — water, caffeine, alcohol — so Ebb replaces the second app you were using for it, and still makes no network calls of its own.</p>

<section class="eb-sec">
  <div class="eb-wrap">
    <p class="eb-eyebrow">The rest of it</p>
    <h2 class="eb-h2">More than four numbers.</h2>
    <div class="eb-how">
      <div>
        <span class="tag">Private log</span>
        <h3>Behind its own gate.</h3>
        <p>An optional, more personal log kept in a separate encrypted store with its own key and a second Face ID gate. Leave it closed forever and nothing else misses it.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <span class="tag">On-device answers</span>
        <h3>Ask, offline.</h3>
        <p>On a supported iPhone, ask a plain question and get an answer written by Apple's on-device model — from your own numbers, never sent anywhere.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <span class="tag">Thresholds</span>
        <h3>Where it turns.</h3>
        <p>It finds where a relationship actually flips — two drinks, not one; caffeine after 2pm — and lags it to the right day, instead of implying less is always better.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="eb-sec" style="padding-bottom:20px;">
  <div class="eb-wrap">
    <p class="eb-eyebrow">Private by design</p>
    <h2 class="eb-h2">Everything stays on this phone.</h2>
    <p class="eb-body">No account, no sync, no analytics — and <b>no networking code at all.</b> Health data is read; the only thing written back is a drink you log. Nothing is ever sent off the phone. It's encrypted at rest and opens with Face ID, and the most personal part — a private log with its own gate and its own key — can be left closed forever without the rest of the app missing it.</p>
    <p class="eb-note">On-device intelligence, when your iPhone supports it: the questions you ask and the weekly note are written by Apple's on-device model, offline. Not medical advice — a mirror, kept to itself.</p>
