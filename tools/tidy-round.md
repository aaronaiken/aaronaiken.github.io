<section class="tr-sec">
  <div class="tr-wrap">
    <p class="tr-eyebrow">What it is</p>
    <h2 class="tr-h2">This is the seven-minute workout.</h2>
    <p class="tr-serif" style="margin-top:20px;max-width:64ch;">It isn't ours. It was published in 2013 by Brett Klika and Chris Jordan in <em>ACSM's Health &amp; Fitness Journal</em> — a circuit that does the work of resistance training and cardio at once, in the time you'd spend deciding whether to go to the gym. We wrote a timer for it, and tried to make it disappear while you use it.</p>

    <div class="tr-three">
      <div>
        <span class="n">01</span>
        <h3>The order isn't arbitrary.</h3>
        <p>A total-body move raises your heart rate first; upper, lower, and core alternate after that, so one region recovers while the next works. The app won't let you shuffle them — shuffling makes it a different workout.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <span class="n">02</span>
        <h3>Effort is the variable.</h3>
        <p>The protocol assumes roughly an 8 out of 10. If you can hold a conversation through it, you're doing something else.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <span class="n">03</span>
        <h3>Seven minutes is the floor.</h3>
        <p>Not the ceiling. One circuit is a real workout; you can run one, two, or three. We'll never tell you which to pick.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="tr-sec">
  <div class="tr-wrap">
    <p class="tr-eyebrow">How it runs</p>
    <h2 class="tr-h2">On the phone, or on the wrist.</h2>
    <div class="tr-runs">
      <div>
        <p class="tr-eyebrow">iPhone</p>
        <h3>Put it down.</h3>
        <p>A 250-point countdown you can read across the room, a contour drawing of each move, and a firm handoff between them. Face-down is the intended posture.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <p class="tr-eyebrow">Apple Watch</p>
        <h3>Run it standalone.</h3>
        <p>The whole circuit from your wrist with the phone off or absent — a haptic at every change, three taps counting you into each finish, and the session saved to your Move and Exercise rings.</p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <p class="tr-note">A wrist-safe circuit is built in, too: lower-body and core only, with your hands open and relaxed — no push-ups, dips, or pressing — for anyone keeping weight off their wrists.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<hr class="tr-rule">

<section class="tr-sec tr-sec--tight">
  <div class="tr-wrap">
    <p class="tr-eyebrow">The shape of it</p>
    <h2 class="tr-h2">Paid for once.</h2>
    <p class="tr-serif" style="margin-top:18px;max-width:60ch;">No account, no subscription, no analytics. The phone and the watch each run their own session and quietly sync the <em>finished</em> ones over your own iCloud — never a live countdown, because syncing that would only make it worse. The phone keeps the whole record; the watch keeps the last couple of months. Nothing goes anywhere else, because there's nowhere else for it to go.</p>
  </div>
</section>
